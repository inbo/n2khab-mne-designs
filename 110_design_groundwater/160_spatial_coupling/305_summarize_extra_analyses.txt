3 Results
=========

3.1 Water Levels
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  Because water levels are herein used only a proxy for water chemistry,
  we chose a rather strict *constraining assumption*: we seek the
  distance at which the difference in water levels is less than or equal
  to \(0.01 m\), i.e. /1 cm/.

  + Sample size per soilclass seems sufficient, even after excluding
    site pairs with more than one percent elevation difference (relative
    to pair distance).
    + We handled a target value of *100 pairs per distance bin*, which
      was reached by variable bin widths.
    + The elevation filter was confirmed to be relevant for stable
      variograms (`116_review_slopefilter.qmd').
  + Difference threshold is hit *at a distance of \(\approx 10 m\)*.
    + `mTAW' and `mMaaiveld', in comparison, allow for nuances in data
      interpretation.
    + *Coupling is highly soilclass dependent.* On heavy soils, the
       range of measurable cross-variation is much shorter, i.e.
       locations are expected to differ at closer distances than on
       light soil and peat.
  + There is a *non-negligible nugget*, i.e. considerable variation even
    at "zero distance".

  We conclude and confirmed that co-variation of water level
  measurements is reasonably tolerable at *a 10 m distance*. Particular
  soilclasses at and a too high elevation difference between candidate
  locations can justify an even more conservative maximum re-use
  distance.


3.2 Water Chemistry
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  Database coding of water chemistry changed some time around 2014,
  which had initially slipped the attention of this author. After
  correction, sufficient paired opservations of water chemistry could be
  obtained.

  + Sample size is just above acceptable minimum with the given
    variance.
    + Variance is high; a bin count of *128 pairs per distance bin*
      seemed reasonably stable for our most well-observed categories.
    + Only measurements on the same calendar day were included. For
      repetitions of the same sites, only the first paired observation
      was selected.
    + Units of milli-equivalents (*MEQ*) were *log-transformed* to
      produce sufficiently-close-to-normal distributed concentration
      data. Difference among two locations in a pair is thus the
      difference in their log10-concentrations.
    + We inspected three chemicals: \(NH_4\), \(NO_3\), and \(PO_4\).
    + This includes measurements which were stored at or below the
      detection limit.

  + "Sigma-ranges" (i.e. distances at which the expected difference
    between two locations exceed half the maximum variation) are
    *generally higher than with water levels*
    (`130_chemistry_variograms.qmd').
    + However, we *did not work with a concentration threshold*, which
      would be quickly met on the "`difference(log10)'" scale.
    + Nuggets are considerable; i.e. baseline variation is high.
    + Only some soilclass/chemical combinations yield meaningful, stable
      variograms. This was confirmed by looking at data distributions
      ("ridgeplots"; `132_chemistry_ridgeplots.qmd').
    + Filtering out pairs in which one concentration was below detection
      limit resulted in unreliable variograms (higher `rmse', more
      variance; `304b_chemistry_variograms_detectionlimit.qmd'). There
      are counteracting effects:
      + Filtering pairs which both display low concentrations removes
        "low-difference" observations and moves the difference-distance
        curve up.
      + However, filtering a pair because of only one critically low
        concentration measurement removes high-difference pairs, moving
        the curve down.
      + Which effect pulls the picture is bin-specific, and in
        consequence removing detection limit observations only leads to
        higher variability / variogram instability.


  To summarize, measurement process characteristics, the scale, and the
  units of water chemistry measurements prohibit conclusive
  interpretation.


3.3 General
~~~~~~~~~~~

  + *Ecoregion:* Filtering for ecoregion indicates what one would
     expect: the better-sampled ecoregions confirm the "global" result,
     whereas others struggle form limited sample size
     (`117_review_ecoregion.qmd',
     `302_chemistry_variograms_ecoregion.qmd')
  + Value tables for other difference thresholds are available in
    notebook `300_spatialcoupling_waterlevels_extract_regressions.qmd'.
    Alternatively, regression parameters can be accessed directly to
    calculate more exact values.
